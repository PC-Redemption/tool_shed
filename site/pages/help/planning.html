Planning

Planning is collaborative investigation that answers, “What are we really trying to build?” It creates only the coordination the work can justify.

Plan from observed reality

Read the repository, current documentation, active work, tests, and delivery boundaries before asserting a path. Keep stable truth in project documentation and unresolved coordination under work/.

ts: orient mets: plan the authentication migration

Choose the minimum sufficient coordination

Direct

A clear, reversible change. Orient once, implement, and run focused checks without creating ceremony.

Guided

Bounded known steps where a checklist or ticket prevents omissions.

Coordinated

Dependencies, branching, multiple sessions, or handoff justify a workpackage or map.

Deep

Consequential cross-layer uncertainty or repeated failure justifies bounded research and controlled evidence.

Artifacts serve the outcome

Checklists prevent forgotten steps. Tickets state a clear behavior change. Workpackages coordinate multi-step transformations. Maps show workstreams. ADRs preserve decisions. Spikes resolve a bounded unknown. Runbooks make an operation repeatable.

Common misuse

Creating an artifact because work feels important, instead of because coordination or future handoff needs it, adds maintenance without improving delivery.

Planning is complete enough when

The outcome, boundaries, important unknowns, evidence needed, and next action are visible. Planning does not need to predict every implementation detail. It should make the next safe action obvious and leave room for evidence to change the path.
