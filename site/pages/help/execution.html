Execution levels

Execution endpoints say where authorized work should stop. They are cumulative, but they do not grant authority outside the goal or make a simple change more complex.

Separate coordination from delivery depth

A Direct change can still be shipped. A deeply coordinated investigation may stop locally. Choose structure based on uncertainty and handoff cost; choose the work level based on the requested delivery endpoint.

An individual workspace may declare ordered actions before or after one canonical endpoint in work/tool-shed.yaml, or explicitly replace that endpoint's standard behavior. Missing customization preserves the defaults.

Five cumulative stopping points

work1 · Local checkpoint

Implement, run the quickest meaningful check, and create a scoped local commit. Do not deploy.

work2 · Work environment

Perform work1, deploy to the configured work environment, and run focused browser and changed-behavior checks.

work3 · Frozen candidate

Align candidate documentation, fully validate and build, update the work environment when relevant, and freeze locally.

work4 · Push

Perform work3 and push without intentionally promoting production.

work5 · Production

Qualify, push, release or promote production, and verify the production target.

Script the routine Work1 and Work2 mechanics

Current Tool Shed snapshots use one guarded runner for deterministic preparation and closeout: identity and state checks, disposable views, change-aware validation, checkpoint/rebuild proof, Doctor, and Work2 release ownership. Product implementation, deployment, target-evidence judgment, and outcome verdicts remain outside the script.

Keep App Server opt-in durable and observable

ts: app-server on makes eligible commands prefer App Server until explicitly disabled, while clean installs remain off and --gui overrides one action. Explicit preference changes refresh a recovery-only owner profile outside Codex home; it never acts as live consent, and destructive replacement requires ts: app-server profile restore.

ts: app-server report summarizes the bounded local opportunity funnel: selections, execution, completion, GUI fallback, reconciliation, failures, and skipped opportunities. Events are content-free and source-aware; validation traffic is isolated and legacy events are excluded rather than reclassified.

Ship means end to end

ts:ship publish the documentation site

The ship route authorizes the applicable plan, implementation, validation, build, deployment, and verification stages for the stated goal. Before a consequential stage, identify credible failure modes and proportionate prevention or rollback. Verify in the target environment before claiming completion.

Common misuse

Do not interpret “build passed” as shipped, or use work5 to bypass credentials, protected-environment controls, repository policy, or a decision that only the owner can make.

Workspace-specific actions

Before execution, Tool Shed resolves aliases to their canonical level and applies one envelope: declared before actions, the standard cumulative endpoint unless explicitly disabled, then declared after actions. Lower-level envelopes do not repeat. Every action is required and execution stops on the first failure.

Configuration cannot waive safety. It cannot broaden the current goal, bypass credentials or approvals, hide production coupling, or make an unverified result complete.

Check without mutation

ts:check focused

The check route validates only. It does not implement, commit, push, deploy, or release. Use it when the question is about the current condition rather than a requested change.
